# Choosing a Welding Process (selection-chart.pdf — image-only source)

The selection chart ("HOW TO CHOOSE A WELDER") compares the four processes. First: **identify your input voltage — 120V or 240V.**

| Question | FLUX-CORED / FCAW | MIG / GMAW | STICK / SMAW | TIG / GTAW |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Skill level | **Low** | **Low** (easiest to learn) | Moderate | **High** |
| Shielding gas | No gas required | **Gas required** (indoor welding recommended) | No gas required | **Gas required** (indoor recommended) |
| Materials | Steel, stainless | Steel, stainless, **aluminum (spool gun required)** | Steel, stainless, castings | Steel, stainless, chrome moly (**DC TIG**); aluminum/magnesium need **AC TIG** (not this machine) |
| Material thickness | 18 gauge to 5/16" | 22 gauge to 3/8" | 10 gauge to 1/2" | 24 gauge to 3/16" |
| Typical applications | Galvanized steel, pipe & tubing, general fabrication, maintenance & repair | Sheet metal, tubing, automotive body, structural steel | Pipe & tubing, structural steel, pressure vessels, maintenance & repair | Stainless exhausts, bicycle frames, thin-wall pipe & tube, metal art |
| Weld cleanliness | More spatter | Clean / minimal spatter | More spatter | **Extremely clean** |

## Process strengths (chart footer)
- **Flux-cored:** ideal outdoors/windy; forgiving on rusty or dirty steel; good out-of-position capability; high deposition rates.
- **MIG:** fast production (high welding speeds); easiest to learn; clean welds, no slag; better control on thin materials.
- **Stick:** ideal outdoors/windy; forgiving on rusty/dirty steel; deep penetration; good for thicker materials.
- **TIG:** highest quality welds; extremely aesthetic appearance; usable on a variety of materials; precise control.

## MIG or Flux-Cored? (chart sidebar)
| Trait | MIG | Flux-cored |
|---|---|---|
| Uses cored wire | — | ✓ |
