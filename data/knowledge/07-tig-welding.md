# TIG Welding (manual pp.24–26, 28–31)

The manual's own notice: **TIG is a complicated process requiring experience — training beyond the manual's scope is needed to TIG weld properly.** [p.30] This machine does **DC TIG** (mild steel, stainless, chrome moly). AC TIG for aluminum is *not* supported. TIG torch and foot pedal are **sold separately**. [pp.7, 24, 28]

## Cable + gas setup (pp.24–25)
1. **Ground Clamp Cable → Positive (+)** socket, twist clockwise to lock.
2. **TIG Torch Cable → Negative (−)** socket, twist clockwise to lock. (DCEN — torch negative.)
3. Foot pedal cable through the front hole → **Foot Pedal Socket inside the welder**, collar clockwise until tight.
4. Gas: secure a **100% Argon** cylinder upright with two straps; crack the valve to blow out dust, close; thread the included Regulator on, wrench-tighten; connect the **shielding gas hose on the TIG torch cable connector** to the regulator outlet, wrench-tighten. (Gas goes to the torch, not through the machine.)
5. Plug the 120V or 240V cord into Power Input — but not into the wall yet.
6. Quick-start reminder: leave wire feed power disconnected for TIG.

## Tungsten electrode prep (p.26)
- Consult the **Settings Chart on top of the welder** for tungsten size vs material thickness.
- Sharpening: dedicate a fine-grit grinding wheel to tungsten only (contamination); wear respirator + ANSI goggles (some electrodes contain hazardous materials, e.g. thoriated). Grind **parallel to the electrode length**, rotating to a blunt point; the conical tip should be **2–2.5× the electrode diameter** long. If contaminated/dulled: snap off the end above the contamination with pliers.
- Assembly: match Collet and Collet Body to the tungsten size → thread collet body into torch front → ceramic nozzle (size appropriate to application) onto collet body → collet in the back → tungsten in from the front → lock with Back Cap. **Tip protrudes 1/8"–1/4" beyond the ceramic nozzle.**

## Welding procedure (pp.30–31)
1. Open the cylinder valve **all the way**; set flow to the settings-chart value — **10–25 SCFH**.
2. Power switch OFF → plug into grounded GFCI 120V (20A) / 240V outlet → torch on non-conductive surface → power ON.
3. Home → Main Control Knob to **TIG** → select. Polarity/gas screen: plug cables per screen, 100% Argon. Set **rod diameter** (Left Knob) and **material thickness** (Right Knob), e.g. 1/16" / 24 Ga.
4. Auto Weld screen: Left Knob = output amperage; **Right Knob = ON energizes the TIG torch** (OCV present — warning).
5. Hold torch in one gloved hand, TIG rod (sold separately) in the other. **Maintain tungsten-to-work distance of 1–1.5× the electrode diameter.**
6. Open the **valve on the TIG torch** to start gas flow.
7. Arc start: push the foot pedal and touch the electrode to the work and lift. (No pedal: touch to work and lift.)
8. When the puddle is hot enough, tilt the torch **10–15° back from vertical**, move it back slightly, and add TIG rod to the front edge of the puddle. Alternate pushing the puddle and adding rod. Remove the rod from the puddle each advance but **keep it inside the gas shield** to avoid oxidation.
9. Finish: release the pedal but keep the torch (gas coverage) over the puddle until it solidifies. Without a pedal: pull the torch away to break the arc, then return gas coverage until solidified.
10. Close the torch valve, turn the Right Knob OFF, torch down on non-conductive surface, power OFF.
11. After use: cool, unplug, remove ground clamp, disconnect torch/ground/pedal, close cylinder valve, remove regulator, cap and store the cylinder.

## Safety specifics (p.30–31)
- Never weld without the grounding clamp. A metal work bench **must be grounded** when TIG welding.
- When not holding the torch it must sit on a non-conductive, nonflammable surface. Only hold the TIG rod with an insulated welding glove.
- Asphyxiation DANGER: shielding gas displaces air; gas without CO2 can knock you out with no feeling of shortness of breath. Ventilate; fix leaks immediately.
- Duty cycle (p.29): 120V 40% @ 125A (100% @ 90A); 240V 30% @ 175A (100% @ 105A). Same thermal-shutdown behavior as wire welding.
- Check progress with the **Strike Test** (p.34).
