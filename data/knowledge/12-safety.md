# Safety Essentials (manual pp.2–6, plus in-context warnings)

Severity words: DANGER = will cause death/serious injury; WARNING = could; CAUTION = minor/moderate; NOTICE = non-injury practice. [p.2]

## Protective gear (pp.18, 28, 3)
Minimum **shade number 10** full face shield or welding mask · ear protection · welding gloves, sleeves, apron · NIOSH-approved respirator · fire-resistant work clothes **without pockets** or cuffs · leather leggings / fire-resistant boots · head covering. Arc rays injure eyes and burn skin. Keep clothing free of grease, oil, solvents.

## Electrical (p.4, 6)
- ELECTRIC SHOCK CAN KILL. Turn off, disconnect power, and discharge the electrode to ground before setting the torch/holder down and before service.
- Never touch energized parts; dry insulating gloves only; don't touch electrode/wire with bare hands.
- Grounded, **GFCI-protected** supply only. Not for use near water/damp areas, in rain, or **outdoors** (p.4 item 8).
- **Pacemaker wearers: consult a physician before use** — EM fields can interfere with pacemakers.
- Insulate yourself from workpiece and ground (dry mats/plywood). Connect per applicable electrical codes.
- **No extension cords.** Never modify grounding; green wire is ground only; no adapter plugs. If the plug doesn't fit, have a proper outlet installed by a qualified electrician.

## Fumes & gas (p.3, 21, 30)
- Welding fumes increase cancer risk (larynx, lung) and are linked to Parkinson's-type effects, heart/kidney disease, etc. Keep your head out of fumes; ventilate or use exhaust at the arc; approved respirator otherwise; NIOSH-approved respirator listed in required PPE.
- Don't weld near degreasing or painting operations.
- **Shielding gas can asphyxiate** — it displaces air; without CO2 you may pass out with no shortness of breath. Ventilate, fix leaks immediately.
- Confined areas: only if well-ventilated or with an air-supplied respirator; industrial-hygiene review for OSHA PELs / ACGIH TLVs.

## Fire (p.4)
- ARC AND SLAG CAUSE FIRE. Clear combustibles within **35 feet (10 m)** or cover them; block openings; ABC extinguisher at hand.
- Metal walls/ceilings: move combustibles on the other side, or post a fire watch during and **at least 30 minutes after** work.
- No welding on materials with combustible coatings/internal structure; don't dump hot slag in containers with combustibles; inspect for fire after welding (smoke/flame may hide for a while).
- Never heat/weld a container that held unknown or combustible substances — clean, purge, and vent first (including castings) before preheating/welding/cutting.
- Not for atmospheres with reactive/flammable liquids, gases, vapors, or dust.

## Cylinders (p.5)
Cylinders can explode. Never weld a pressurized/closed cylinder; never let torch/electrode/wire touch a cylinder; keep cylinders away from all electrical circuits; cap on except in use; protect from heat, strikes, slag, flames, sparks, arcs; move properly.

## Use & care (p.5)
- Don't use if the power switch is broken. Unplug before adjustments, accessory changes, storage. Prevent unintentional starting. Store away from children; untrained users are dangerous. Maintain labels/nameplates. Qualified repair with identical parts only. **Not for pipe thawing.**
- Never leave the welder unattended while energized; don't operate tired or impaired.

## Situational warnings the agent should surface
- Setup: power OFF + unplugged before wire/roller work (p.10); parts at welding voltage during feed — door closed unless using Cold Wire Feed, never touch the trigger while feeding (p.16).
- The welder is energized with OCV present once the TIG torch / electrode holder is turned ON via the right knob (pp.30, 32).
- Rest guns/holders/torches on non-conductive, heat-proof surfaces away from the ground clamp (pp.19, 23, 29–33).
- Vehicle work: disconnect the vehicle battery (both terminals) before welding on it (pp.19, 29).
- Keep welding tip clear of grounded objects whenever plugged in and on (pp.18, 28).
- Symbology (p.6): IPM = inches/minute wire speed; AWG = wire gauge; OCV = open-circuit voltage; KVA = volts×amps/1000; icons for shock, inhalation, fire, arc-ray, pacemaker hazards.
